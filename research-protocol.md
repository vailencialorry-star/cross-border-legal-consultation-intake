# Research Protocol

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. Statutes, regulations, treaty repositories, tax administrations, customs, courts, financial regulators and data-protection authorities.
2. Binding or authoritative judicial decisions and official guidance.
3. Regulator FAQs, filing/licensing manuals and administrative notices.
4. Reputable secondary commentary only to identify issues or sources.

Open and read the official source. Never cite a search-result snippet as evidence.

For foreign law, prefer the official-language current text. An official translation can explain the rule but must be labelled non-authoritative when the publisher says so.

## Currency check

For each material source record:

- Title and issuing body.
- Provision or section.
- Effective or update date.
- Whether the page shows current, amended, repealed or future status.
- URL.
- Any limitation on translation or territorial scope.

If the user's date is later than a source's update date, state that the source is the latest official version located, not guaranteed law as of a future date.

## Jurisdiction research schema

Return comparable fields:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Bottom line | Launchable, conditional or defer/prohibit conclusions |
| Issue matrix | Topic, rule, authority, application, risk and action |
| Contract/property | Role, title, delivery, third party, insolvency and consumer issues |
| Licensing | Platform, warehouse, secondhand, auction, payment and local filing |
| Tax/customs | Supply, income, invoice, import/export, valuation and evidence |
| AML/criminal | Status, reporting, proceeds, stolen goods, red flags and controls |
| Data/evidence | Collection, overseas transfer, retention and electronic evidence |
| Treaty points | Conflict law, sales convention, trade agreement, tax treaty and execution |
| Unresolved | Exact local opinion, ruling, licence or external confirmation required |
| Official sources | Title, authority, URL, provision and currency note |

## Cross-border synthesis rules

### Contract vs. property

Analyze contract governing law separately from proprietary title, possession, delivery, third-party effectiveness, insolvency and attachment. Start with the conflict rule of each connected jurisdiction and the law of the asset's location.

### Sale convention

For CISG or another sales convention, check:

- Contracting-state status and reservations.
- B2B vs. consumer use.
- Goods vs. predominant services.
- Express exclusion or incorporation.
- Matters outside scope, especially validity and effect on title.

### Trade agreement

A trade agreement does not create origin merely because goods are purchased, graded, stored, relabelled or resold in a member country. Check the exact HS code, product-specific rule, production facts, direct consignment rule and proof of origin.

### Tax treaty

A tax treaty generally addresses income taxes, PE, withholding or relief. Do not use it to answer VAT/GST, customs, import tax, licensing, property, data or consumer questions. Check protocol, MLI or later modification and principal-purpose/anti-abuse rules.

### Dispute enforcement

Separate:

- Governing law.
- Court or arbitral jurisdiction.
- Emergency relief where the asset is located.
- Recognition and enforcement.
- Consumer restrictions.

For arbitration, verify treaty status and reservations; keep local-court relief for custody, attachment and return of physical assets.

## Risk and decision language

Use this formulation:

> **Rule:** [current legal rule]. **Application:** [why facts fall within or outside it]. **Preliminary conclusion:** [launchable/conditional/defer]. **Required action:** [specific document, control, ruling, licence, local opinion or external confirmation].

Do not use “may involve” as the final recommendation. If law is uncertain, identify the precise uncertainty and the person or authority who can resolve it.

## Minimum red-flag areas for high-value movable property platforms

Always test:

- Unverified or stolen title.
- Rapid repeated transfers, loops and linked accounts.
- Material value deviation without business reason.
- Third-party payer, recipient or withdrawal address.
- Long custody with repeated ownership changes.
- Cash, crypto, wallet, internal netting or off-platform top-up.
- False personal-use import, undervaluation, misclassification or split consignments.
- Platform control of funds despite a PSP label.
- Cross-border KYC, payment, device and asset-source data sharing.
